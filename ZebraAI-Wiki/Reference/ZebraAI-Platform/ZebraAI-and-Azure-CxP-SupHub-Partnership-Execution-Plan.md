# Summary

This document outlines the tactical execution plan for the strategic
partnership between ZebraAI and Azure CxP Supportability Hub aimed at
unifying AI experimentation with enterprise-scale support operations to
enhance efficiency, reduce costs, and improve user experience.

- **Unified Platform Integration:** ZebraAI’s interface will be
  integrated into the Supportability Hub portal, consolidating AI
  experimentation capabilities within a single platform to eliminate
  tool switching and improve user experience. Early pilots have
  validated this approach with phased rollouts planned for scalability.

- **Production-Scale Backend Transition:** ZebraAI’s backend and data
  pipelines will migrate to Supportability Hub’s infrastructure to
  achieve 99.9% uptime and full compliance with Microsoft security and
  privacy standards, enabling enterprise-grade reliability and
  operational support.

- **Measurable Support Impact:** The partnership aims to reduce support
  costs through efficiency gains and improve KPIs such as self-help
  deflection rates and case resolution times by deploying AI-powered
  solutions quarterly through FY26, tracking impact via impact
  assessment models.

- **Collaborative Governance Model:** By end of FY26Q1, a joint
  governance framework between CSS and Azure CxP will be established to
  ensure shared ownership, transparency, and coordinated
  decision-making, supported by regular sprint alignments and governance
  meetings.

- **User Adoption and Engagement:** Adoption will be driven through
  phased rollouts, training, and an expanded Ambassador program,
  targeting a 50% increase in monthly active users and raising
  experiment creators from 22% to 40% by end of FY26, with user
  satisfaction as a key success metric.

- **Scope of Integration:** The project encompasses AI services
  accessible via APIs, Azure infrastructure management, data platform
  migration ensuring compliance with EU and US data residency
  requirements, front-end UI integration evolving from Iframe embedding
  to native React components, user community enablement, security and
  compliance measures, and comprehensive testing.

- **Out of Scope Areas:** The project excludes modifications to existing
  Supportability Hub features unrelated to ZebraAI, external user access
  beyond internal Microsoft users, creation of new datasets beyond
  existing data used by ZebraAI, and long-term post-launch operations
  beyond initial support and handover.

- **Detailed Execution Plan and Milestones:** A phased timeline is
  presented covering planning, UI embedding, data migration, backend
  cutover, AI feature rollout, and full native UI deployment with
  defined deliverables, acceptance criteria, and stakeholder
  communications.

- **Governance, Communication, and Risk Management:** The partnership
  includes joint project management, a RACI matrix defining roles and
  responsibilities, monthly business reviews with executive sponsors, a
  comprehensive communication plan for all stakeholders, and a risk log
  addressing compliance, integration, AI performance, resource
  constraints, timeline, and user adoption risks with mitigation
  strategies.

# Objectives

Objectives in this execution plan document define the specific,
measurable outcomes the partnership seeks to achieve through integration
and transformation efforts. They establish the intended impact of the
project such as enhancing user experience, improving operational
reliability and compliance, reducing support costs, and fostering
effective collaboration and guide all phases of planning, execution, and
evaluation. By clearly stating these objectives, the plan ensures all
stakeholders maintain a shared understanding of success criteria and can
collectively track progress toward the desired results.

## ZebraAI and Supportability Hub Unified Platform

Enhance the user experience by integrating ZebraAI’s interface into the
Supportability Hub portal, enabling users to access AI experimentation
capabilities within a single, unified platform. This consolidation will
serve all users from both platforms, facilitating seamless
experimentation with AI solutions and their operationalization without
the need to switch between systems. Establishing a unified environment
lays the groundwork for future development phases and delivers immediate
improvements to user experience. Implementation will occur in planned
phases to ensure that users of both ZebraAI and Supportability Hub are
well-prepared and fully informed about the integration process. An
initial pilot integration has already demonstrated the feasibility of
this approach. The subsequent steps involve a controlled rollout to a
select group of users for further evaluation.

## Production Scale with Security First Approach

To strengthen enterprise-level reliability, security, compliance, and operational support, the ZebraAI team is partnering with the Supportability Hubs team to evolve our backend and data pipelines. This collaboration aligns ZebraAI with Supportability Hub’s engineering, monitoring, and operations standards, enabling us to collectively deliver robust and scalable solutions. By leveraging Support Hub’s knowledge and operational excellence, more capacity can be applied to advancing AI capabilities. The collaboration will begin with data pipeline migration and EUDB onboarding, followed by the remaining ZebraAI Azure services and platform. Throughout this process, we remain fully committed to Microsoft’s security, compliance, privacy, Responsible AI (RAI), and SFI standards. All required compliance milestones, including TRiP reviews, threat modeling, accessibility, penetration testing, and 1CS assessments will be completed and documented with rigor and transparency.

## Measurable Support Impact

ZebraAI and Supportability Hub aim to reduce support effort and costs by
creating a unified AI experimentation platform that improves customer
support and operational efficiency. Integrating ZebraAI will boost
self-help rates, automate processes, and enhance support engineer
productivity, leading to better KPIs such as higher deflection rates and
faster case resolution. The project targets measurable progress
throughout FY26, including quarterly deployment of AI-powered solutions
and increased savings. Financial impact will be tracked through impact
assessment and A/B models, with cost reduction achieved via scalable
automation and streamlined customer self-service. Previous successes,
like automated language translation, demonstrate significant savings,
and phased rollouts will help ensure each innovation meets its goals.
Besides Supportability Hub, CaseBuddy will be our primary endpoint for
surfacing impactful AI solutions. Endpoints like DfM/DfC, Agent Copilot,
Copilot Hub, and others are still important, but CaseBuddy provides the
perfect interface for accelerated, iterative innovation.

## Collaborative Governance

Create a collaborative partnership where CSS and Azure CxP operate as a
unified team, making joint decisions and solving problems together. This
approach builds transparency and trust, ensures shared ownership of the
integrated platform, and enables quick responses to challenges. To
support this, implement joint planning, shared accountability, and clear
communication channels, such as bi-weekly sprint alignments, monthly
governance meetings, and a unified roadmap and backlog. Leadership from
both sides have committed to integration, emphasizing early alignment
across teams to avoid duplication. With executive backing and an
operational partnership forming, establish a joint governance model by
end of FY26Q2, including a shared scorecard, regular cross-team
meetings, and a documented RACI. Success will be measured by
cross-functional milestone delivery and co-owned decision-making.

## User Adoption & Engagement

The project will encourage adoption through a phased rollout utilizing
ZebraAI ambassadors for outreach. In FY25, the ZebraAI user community
grew from 2.4k to over 13k users after new capabilities were introduced,
indicating that demonstrated value drives usage. Integrating ZebraAI
into the Supportability Hub should boost engagement by reducing
fragmentation and improving accessibility for all users. Training,
events, office hours, and the Ambassador network will help transition
users to the unified platform. Meeting adoption targets is crucial;
neither ZebraAI or Supportability Hub should lose users nor see a
reduction in usage. Higher adoption will foster more experimentation and
innovations, enhancing platform content and features. The goal is to
ensure the integrated platform meets user needs and achieves high levels
utilization.

# Scope

## In-Scope

| AI Services & Solutions | The development of ZebraAI’s core AI functionalities will focus on ensuring integration through APIs. This involves building AI services and implementing core Microsoft AI technologies, with these services accessible via well-defined APIs and a middleware layer managing business logic and workflow orchestration between the AI backend and the front-end portal. The ZebraAI team will maintain and enhance these AI capabilities and APIs in accordance with the partnership plan. For instance, the project will expand ZebraAI’s existing API framework, which currently supports integrations with tools such as DfM or CaseBuddy to enable direct calls from Supportability Hub. This scope item is intended to make the platform’s AI functions reliable and accessible, allowing Supportability Hub and other internal tools to utilize ZebraAI’s features via the middleware. The approach is designed to support the objective of delivering unified solutions. Additionally, the ZebraAI team will develop end-to-end solutions utilizing new AI capabilities. |
|----|----|
| Azure Resources | Azure resources will continue to reside within the current ZebraAI subscriptions, with the option to explore new services within the Supportability Hub’s managed Azure environment as future needs evolve. The Supportability Hub team will begin supporting ZebraAI resources following a collaborative knowledge transfer and training period, during which both teams will align on access provisioning and operational practices. Ownership of the Service Tree and IcM will be shared between ZebraAI and Supportability Hub. While Supportability Hub will take the lead on off-hours on-call responsibilities, they will engage the ZebraAI team as needed for consultation on unresolved or complex issues. This joint approach ensures continuity and responsiveness across all hours. By the end of the transition, ZebraAI’s backend will operate as a fully integrated part of the Supportability Hub ecosystem, benefiting from unified telemetry, compliance, and monitoring to support production-scale readiness and reliability. Operational monitoring and support processes will be set up for the integrated platform. We will document procedures (alerting, dashboards, on-call rotations) and manage operations throughout. |
| Data Platform | All data pipelines and storage will be migrated to the Supportability Hub platform's data ingestion pipelines and storage facilities. For instance, case data pipelines that support ZebraAI experiments will transition to the Supportability Hub data platform. Separate data instances for EU and non-EU data will be configured to address data residency requirements. Compliance with EU Data Boundaries (EUDB) falls within the scope of this project. An EU-compliant data pipeline will be established so that sensitive European data is processed within the region. The data pipeline will also accommodate US Federal, Government, and DoD data handling requirements. The scope includes implementing necessary data integrations to incorporate relevant data into the AI platform (such as GetHelp, VBD, LivePerson, additional Cornerstone data, etc.). All data handling processes will adhere to internal compliance, privacy, and security standards. Current ZebraAI data providers will be informed about this partnership to facilitate smooth collaboration. Revised data contracts with data providers will result from this transition. Upon completion of the project, all ZebraAI data will be managed and stored in the Supportability Hub data platform. |
| Front-End Integration | To unify the user experience, ZebraAI’s interface will be integrated into the Supportability Hub portal. Initially, ZebraAI's web UI will be embedded via an Iframe within the Supportability Hub, allowing users access alongside other support tools. Next, ZebraAI will provide embeddable UI components for direct integration into the Supportability Hub’s React-based portal, including seamless navigation and authentication using existing credentials. The goal is to evolve from basic embedding to a native React integration that adopts the Supportability Hub's design language, with both teams collaborating on UI/UX. User feedback during testing will help refine the interface, ensuring ZebraAI feels like a built-in feature—not a separate tool—delivering a cohesive portal experience. |
| User Community & Enablement | Active user communities and ambassadors are key to this project's success. The existing ZebraAI community and ambassador program will drive adoption and ensure users engage with the platform beyond its technical launch. The project includes developing a support forum or Teams channel for discussions, updating self-help resources like FAQs and how-to guides, and expanding the Ambassador Program to recruit and train advocates across teams and regions. Ambassadors will help onboard users, host demos, and provide feedback. Internal events like workshops and office hours will promote new features. The goal is for users to have the knowledge and support they need to adopt the platform when it launches. |
| Security & Compliance Measures | Security and compliance will be integrated throughout the project, prioritizing a “Security First” approach. This includes implementing access controls (e.g., Azure AD single sign-on, Core Identity, role-based permissions), encrypting all data in transit and at rest, and ensuring adherence to all relevant privacy regulations such as GDPR. Compliance tasks include completing SFI and SDL documentation, establishing monitoring and audit logs, and performing privacy and accessibility reviews before launch. The Supportability Hub team will take on ongoing compliance responsibilities once ZebraAI is on their platform, while both teams will work together during the project to ensure full compliance with required standards and assessments. Regular security reviews and penetration testing will address vulnerabilities early. The goal is to launch with no outstanding security or compliance gaps, delivering a trustworthy and compliant solution. |
| Testing and QA | Comprehensive testing will be performed to verify the quality and reliability of the integrated solution. The project scope includes end-to-end quality assurance activities for each major deliverable. This encompasses unit testing of new code (such as AI service functions, API endpoints, and UI components) by developers, along with integration testing to confirm that ZebraAI components operate correctly within Supportability Hub. The plan also includes performance and load testing on the Azure backend, which involves simulating increased usage to assess scalability and adherence to performance SLAs (such as a 99.9% uptime and responsive performance targets). User Acceptance Testing (UAT) will be carried out by deploying to a pilot group of users (for example, Ambassadors or another selected user group) before a wider rollout. During UAT, feedback on functionality will be collected and any identified issues or usability concerns will be addressed. The creation of test plans, development of test cases, and management of bug tracking are included in this scope. Integration will not be considered complete until these tests demonstrate that core scenarios function reliably. For instance, the process requires that a user can run an AI experiment on a support case and receive a valid result without errors, and that an AI-generated solution is able to be published through the standard workflow. The FY26H1 success criteria require at least two end-to-end scenarios to be operational and validated in production; as such, testing of these scenarios from experiment initiation to automated action is included as a deliverable. Overall, all validation activities intended to confirm the project deliverables meet acceptance criteria are within scope. This approach supports the objective of implementing a technical solution that performs as specified and maintains current support processes. |

listed in scope is by definition out-of-scope, but the following

boundaries:

# Workstreams

In the AIT ZebraAI and Azure CxP Supportability Hub Partnership
Execution Plan, a workstream refers to a focused set of coordinated
activities and deliverables within a complex initiative. Each workstream
addresses a critical aspect, such as software development,
infrastructure migration, or compliance—and helps ensure progress in
line with overall objectives. This structure divides the project into
manageable segments, each with clear ownership and accountability,
allowing teams to concentrate expertise and resources where needed. By
organizing responsibilities into workstreams, the partnership enables
efficient integration, encourages innovation, and supports ongoing
operational excellence.

Each workstream is characterized by:

- A clearly defined domain of work (e.g., AI Web Services & Middleware,
  Azure Backend & Data Platform, etc.).

- Specific objectives and deliverables that contribute directly to
  project milestones and success metrics.

- Dedicated leadership and team **membership**, often drawing on
  expertise from both the ZebraAI and Supportability Hub teams.

# Roles & Responsibilities

The RACI matrix will be maintained throughout the project for all major
deliverables and milestones. If project scope changes or new tasks are
added, the RACI will be updated so every task has clear areas of
responsibility. This clarity in role designation means each team member
knows their responsibilities upfront, thereby preventing confusion and
keeping the joint ZebraAI–Supportability Hub efforts running
smoothly. Regular reviews will ensure the matrix remains accurate. By
clearly defining “who does what”, the project can leverage the combined
strengths of the ZebraAI and Supportability Hub teams without ambiguity
and escalate decisions efficiently to the Executive Sponsors when
needed. This approach sustains strong support for the project across
both organizations and helps deliver a solution that meets all parties’
needs with no surprises.

This shared **joint development model** leverages each team’s strengths:

- ZebraAI Team (CSS) – Responsible for current platform, data pipeline,
  AI models, and experimentation capabilities.  They also lead the
  ZebraAI user community and Ambassador program and create self-help
  content, steering the overall AI product roadmap.

- Supportability Hub Team (Azure CxP) – Responsible for cloud
  infrastructure, data platform, and operational excellence. They own
  the Supportability Hub experience and related services (case review,
  solution authoring, etc.) and manage all required Azure resources
  (databases, storage, cognitive services). They ensure secure,
  scalable, and compliant infrastructure, handle deployment pipelines,
  performance, and reliability in production. They will lead ZebraAI’s
  transition into enterprise-grade infrastructure and processes.

- Joint Collaboration – Some areas (especially front-end integration,
  security compliance, and roadmap alignment) require both teams to work
  together. Front-end user experience is co-developed to blend ZebraAI
  into Supportability Hub’s UI, requiring close collaboration on design
  and coding. Security & compliance is a shared effort, with both teams
  ensuring all standards are met (threat models, privacy, RAI,
  etc.). Both teams will jointly define the integrated roadmap and key
  results (KRs), aligning deliverables for measurable
  impact. Throughout, Project Managers from each side coordinate to
  maintain a unified plan.

# Monthly Business Reviews (MBR)

Monthly Business Reviews (MBRs) are formal, recurring checkpoints with
executive sponsors and leadership from both ZebraAI (CSS) and Azure CxP
Supportability Hub teams. The purpose is to provide a fair and accurate
assessment of the project’s progress against plans, surface any issues,
and ensure both teams remain aligned on next steps. By holding these
reviews regularly, we maintain accountability on both sides of the
partnership and get executive support to clear roadblocks promptly.
Below is a detailed MBR framework tailored to our ZebraAI–Supportability
Hub integration project.

## MBR Purpose and Objectives

The MBR meetings serve as a top-level progress report and alignment
forum. Key objectives of the MBR include:

- Shared Understanding of Status: Ensure both teams (and leadership)
  have a consistent view of where the project stands relative to
  goals. This involves presenting factual progress data (metrics,
  milestone status) so everyone agrees on the reality of performance.

- Course Correction & Priority Setting: Discuss whether the current
  direction needs adjustment. Strategic questions are asked, such as “Is
  this goal still important? Are we measuring success with the right
  metric? What’s our progress, and if we’re behind, who is responsible
  for the fix?” These open-ended questions prompt honest evaluation of
  each goal and metric. If priorities have shifted or if approach
  changes are needed, this is the forum to agree on them with executive
  input.

- Reconcile Perspectives: Provide a venue for both teams to reconcile
  any differences in perspective. For example, the ZebraAI team might
  feel a feature is on track while the Supportability Hub side has
  concerns; the MBR brings those views together to reach a mutual
  understanding of overall effectiveness.

- Clear Roadblocks: Identify blockers or decisions that require
  leadership intervention. The MBR is crucial for raising issues that
  cannot be solved by the project team alone and getting executive
  sponsorship to remove obstacles (e.g. needing additional Azure budget
  or an approval from another org). Because leadership from each side is
  present, decisions can be made quickly in the meeting to keep the
  project moving.

- Maintain Accountability: By reviewing commitments and metrics, the MBR
  holds each owner accountable for their part. Each action item from the
  prior MBR is followed up, ensuring nothing fell through the cracks. If
  a deliverable slipped, the responsible owner must explain why and
  present a get-well plan.

- Celebrate Wins: MBRs aren’t just about problems; they also highlight
  achievements. Recognizing key milestones completed, or individuals who
  contributed significantly in the last period, helps maintain team
  morale and demonstrates to leadership the positive momentum. This
  “celebrate wins” aspect is important for partnership health, showing
  both sides are contributing, and progress is being made.

## Attendees

The MBR will include senior stakeholders from both organizations:

- **Executive Sponsors:** The CSS/ZebraAI and the Azure CxP
  Supportability Hub executive sponsors. Their presence signals the
  importance of the project. They will listen to the updates, ask
  high-level strategic questions, and make final decisions if teams have
  differing opinions (as agreed: in case of differences, the Executive
  Sponsors will make the call to resolve disputes).

- **Project Leadership:** The core project leaders (Project Managers
  from our team) who prepare and present most of the content. They
  provide detailed status updates and facilitate the discussion.

- **Technical Leads:** Key engineering leads from ZebraAI and
  Supportability Hub to answer any detailed technical questions and to
  report on technical progress/issues.

- **Optional Stakeholders:** Only as needed, we might include
  representatives from specific stakeholder groups if a particular risk
  or topic is on the agenda. For example, if a security issue is being
  discussed, we could invite the security/compliance officer for that
  portion. However, to keep the MBR focused, we’ll generally limit
  attendance to about 10-15 people (senior stakeholders and core team)
  rather than inviting everyone.

### Cadence

We will hold the MBR monthly. This frequency aligns with typical
business rhythms and gives enough time for measurable progress between
meetings. Since the whole project spans ~6-9 months, that means roughly
6-9 MBR sessions. We will schedule all of them in advance so it’s on
everyone’s calendars and they can plan around the meetings.  Generally,
monthly is sufficient unless executive sponsors or leadership request an
interim check-in.

### Standard MBR Agenda

Each MBR will use a consistent agenda to cover all necessary topics
systematically. A standard agenda (approximately 60 minutes) might look
like this:

1. **Introduction & Recap (5 min):** A brief restatement of the project
    vision and current phase objectives. This ensures everyone not
    involved daily are reminded of context. If the meeting includes new
    participants, we’d also quickly introduce attendees. This sets the
    stage and ties the review to the bigger picture goals.

2. **Overall Status Update (5 min):** A high-level status summary using
    R/A/G (**R**ed/**A**mber/**G**reen) indicators for key areas of the
    project:

    - **Schedule** – are we on track or are there timeline
      slippages? Show the major milestones timeline and mark any that
      changed.

    - **Scope** – any scope changes or change requests since last
      time? If everything is within the original scope, it’s Green. If
      there’s a proposed new feature or de-scoping under discussion,
      mark Amber and mention it.

    - **Resources** – note any resource constraints. If within
      tolerance, Green; if additional resourcing required, maybe Amber
      or Red depending on severity.

    - **Project Health** – an overall project RAG. Green if all major
      things are on track, Amber if there are some concerns, and Red if
      there’s a serious issue threatening success. This section is a
      quick snapshot.

3. **Key Progress & Achievements (since last MBR) (10 min):** Highlight
    the major accomplishments in the last month:

    - Focus on deliverables completed or major milestones achieved.

    - Mention any notable results or feedback.

    - If appropriate, do a quick demo or show visuals of completed or
      new capabilities/features. Demos keep the meeting lively and let
      leadership see tangible progress.

    - Celebrates wins and show momentum, which is important for
      maintaining executive support and team morale.

4. **Metrics Review (10 min):** Present the key metrics/KPIs we are
    tracking to measure project success. This grounds the discussion in
    quantifiable facts. We will likely show a table or dashboard of
    metrics such as usage, quality, and outcomes:

    - **Development Progress:** Sprint velocity trend or other key
      sprint related metrics.

    - **Quality metrics***:* Performance, user feedback, etc.

    - **Adoption metrics:** Usage, active users, etc.

    - **Support impact:** if available, link to desired KPIs like
      resolution time reduction or efficiency gains.

5. **Top Risks & Blockers (10min):** A candid discussion of the most
    pressing risks, issues, or blockers. We will list the top 3-5
    risks/blockers and for each, state:

    - **Description & Impact:** pulled directly from the risk log
      maintained in this document.

    - **Owner:** who is responsible for resolving it.

    - **Mitigation or Ask:** what is being done or what help is
      needed. Possibly flag if leadership decision/support is needed.

      - This is arguably the most critical part of the MBR: leadership
        expects to hear about problems here, not just good news. By
        surfacing these, we invite them to help.

      - If any risk moved to issue (occurring problem), report on the
        recovery plan.

      - All risks on the list will tie back to our risk log maintained
        in this document but in the MBR we focus only on the most urgent
        ones.

6. **Discussion & Decisions (Q&A) (15 min):** Open the floor for the
    executive sponsors and leaders to ask questions or drill deeper into
    any topic. If any decisions are proposed, executive sponsors will
    confer and decide here. They may discuss pros/cons and then say yes
    or no. We will document any decisions made.

7. **Action Items & Owners (5-10 min):** Before closing, we recap any
    new action items that came out of the meeting. Each action is
    clearly assigned to an owner and given a due date.

AI experimentation capabilities into the Supportability Hub, improving
support efficiency, reducing costs, and enabling seamless AI-driven
workflows for support engineers. This partnership aims to operationalize
AI experiments, enhance user experience, and deliver measurable business
